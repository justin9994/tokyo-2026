旅DRAFT

Read this first. Our dates sit on top of Shōgatsu, Japan's Christmas-and-Thanksgiving rolled together. From about Dec 30 to Jan 3 most independent restaurants, many museums, the Tsukiji fish market and department stores close. Jan 1–3, millions of people queue at shrines for hatsumode. It's a special time to be there, but you plan around it: eat and shop hard on Dec 29–30, party on Dec 31, ride the quiet on Jan 1, then hit Uji, Disney and the good restaurants once everything reopens Jan 4–7.

Land, drop bags, look down on Shinjuku for free

Narita 16:40 → hotel by ~19:30 → free 202 m observation deck next door → yakitori alley dinner.
arrivaleasy night
First night after an 12-hour flight and a 17-hour time jump. Zero commitments, everything within a 10-minute walk of the hotel, in bed by 23:00.
  • Airport → hotel. Immigration at Narita can take an hour at 17:00. Then the Airport Limousine Bus (~¥3,600, 100 min, stops at the Nishi-Shinjuku hotels) or Narita Express to Shinjuku (~¥3,250, 80 min) plus a short taxi. Bus wins with four people and luggage.
  • Before landing: add a Suica card to your phone wallet. It pays for every train, bus, vending machine and konbini.
  • Tokyo Metropolitan Government Building observatory (locals say "Tocho"): 202 m up, free, the building next to the hotel, south deck open to 22:00. This is the Shibuya Sky view without the ¥3,400 ticket or the queue.
  • Dinner at Omoide Yokocho, a 1940s alley of tiny grilled-skewer bars by Shinjuku station. Kabuto for eel, anything with a line for chicken. Cash. Dec 26–28 is bonenkai season so bounce between counters.
  • Wiped out: Isetan department store's basement food hall (depachika) closes 20:00; grab a spread and eat in the suite.

Fish market dash, Ginza watches, teamLab at night

Tsukiji by 8:00 (last day it's open) → Ginza vintage watch shops → teamLab Planets timed slot → first fine-dining night.
NYbookjet-lag friendly
You'll be awake at 5 a.m. anyway. Tsukiji Outer Market closes for New Year from about Dec 30 to Jan 4, so today is the only shot, and it's also the market's busiest week of the year. Go early, eat standing, leave by 10.
  • Tsukiji Outer Market, 8:00. The wholesale fish auction moved to Toyosu in 2018; what's left is 400 street stalls: tuna bowls, tamagoyaki on a stick, grilled scallops, strawberry daifuku. ¥3,000–5,000 pp to graze.
  • Ginza, 15 min walk. Shellman (vintage watch specialist since 1971), Grand Seiko flagship, the Seiko clock tower, Seiko Museum (free, book a slot). Dover Street Market, a 12-floor Uniqlo, Itoya stationery. Cafe de l'Ambre for coffee in a 1948 kissaten.
  • teamLab Planets (Toyosu, ~¥3,200–3,600, timed ticket). Digital art you walk through barefoot, some rooms are knee-deep water. Roll-up pants. It closes for good at the end of 2027, which is why it beats Borderless.
  • Fine-dining night #1. Sazenka (1 Michelin star Chinese kaiseki, bookable instantly online) or Manten Sushi (¥8,800 flat omakase, seats four easily). Many top places already start their holiday closure tomorrow.

Yoyogi Park run + coffee → Cat Street brands → Shibuya view (paid or free) → last fine-dining night before closures → Bar Benfiddich.
NYbook
The last normal day before Japan shuts down. Spend it in the two neighborhoods everyone comes to Tokyo for, and use the evening for the restaurant that costs the most, because from tomorrow it's closed until Jan 4.

Old Tokyo at dawn, onsen, then Circus Tokyo till 5

Senso-ji temple at 7:00 → knife street → hot spring recovery → early dinner, nap → Circus Tokyo 22:00–05:00.
NYEtickets
Tonight is the reason we're here on these dates. The day is built to be low-effort and to get everyone horizontal for two hours before 22:00.
  • Senso-ji, Asakusa, 7:00. Tokyo's oldest temple. At 7 it's monks and pigeons; by 10 it's a crowd, and tonight it hosts the Joya no Kane bells. Walk Nakamise street as the shutters open.
  • Kappabashi, 15 min walk: the street where restaurants buy their kitchens. Kama-Asa for knives with free name engraving. Tax-free with passport.
  • Onsen recovery at Thermae-Yu in Kabukicho (24 h, ~¥2,700). Tattoos are allowed if covered with their ¥300 stickers, max two per person, so this only works for small tattoos. Tattoo-friendly alternatives: Kogane-yu or Daikoku-yu in Kinshicho, neighborhood bathhouses (sento) with a craft beer bar, ¥520–1,000.
  • Early dinner at a hotel restaurant or a chain; most independents are closed. Nap.
  • Circus Tokyo, 5 min from Shibuya station's New South exit. Two rooms, high-ceilinged basement, house/techno/breaks/DnB. Last year's countdown ran 22:00–05:00, London Elektricity headlining, ¥3,500 early bird to ¥5,000 door, on ZAIKO and Resident Advisor. This year's lineup lands late Oct to Nov. Bring passports (20+ and ID checked).
  • If the lineup disappoints: WOMB (Shibuya techno institution, ¥6,000–8,000), Zerotokyo (Kabukicho, 1,500 people, five floors, walk home).
  • Home: some trains run all night on NYE; otherwise a taxi Shibuya → hotel is ~¥3,000. Ichiran ramen in Shibuya is open at 5 a.m.

Nothing day, on purpose

Sleep → hotel gym → small neighborhood shrine → New Year box meal at the hotel.
everything closed
Jan 1 in Japan is like Christmas morning in the US: shuttered and quiet. Department stores, most restaurants and banks are closed. It's also the morning after a 5 a.m. club exit. Lean into it.
  • Konbini breakfast (the 7-Eleven egg sandwich is a legit thing). Hotel gym when you surface.
  • Hatsumode, small version. Meiji Jingu will see 3 million people over three days. Hanazono Shrine, 15 min walk in Shinjuku, gives you the same ritual with festival food stalls at a tenth of the scale. Toss a coin, pull an omikuji fortune.
  • Osechi at the hotel: osechi is the traditional New Year meal, ordered weeks ahead. Ask the Hyatt concierge in October.
  • Konbini ATMs work; bank ATMs may not until Jan 4.

See the Emperor, buy a mystery bag, run the palace

Imperial Palace 5 km run → the Imperial Family's balcony appearance → fukubukuro lucky bags at Isetan → Marunouchi lights.
NYonly happens Jan 2
Two things exist only on this date: the Imperial Family greets the public from the palace balcony, and stores reopen with fukubukuro. Both are very Japanese, both free-ish, both near each other.
  • Imperial Palace loop, the 5 km run every Tokyo runner does, counter-clockwise around the moat. adidas Runbase lockers and showers, ¥800.
  • New Year Greeting: the Emperor and family appear on the palace balcony about five times between roughly 10:10 and 14:20 (2027 times to be confirmed). Free, enter through the main gate, they hand you a paper flag. Expect airport-style bag checks and 30–60 min of queueing.
  • Fukubukuro: Isetan Shinjuku opens 10:00 on Jan 2, Lumine 11:00. People queue hours for Beams and Uniqlo bags. Winter sales start the same day.
  • Marunouchi illuminations (through Feb 15) then Nemuro Hanamaru conveyor-belt sushi at Kitte, Hokkaido fish, ¥3,000 pp, always a line, worth it.

Snowboard day at GALA Yuzawa

Bullet train 77 min → gondola at the station exit → ride → onsen at the station → home for dinner.
triptrain + liftgo/no-go Dec 27
Option A from the group: a zero-logistics snow day with no overnight. GALA is the only resort in Japan built on top of its own shinkansen station. It's cruiser terrain, not Niseko powder, but it's real snow 77 minutes from Tokyo Station. Restaurants and clubs are still shut in Tokyo today anyway.
  • Train: Joetsu Shinkansen Tokyo → GALA Yuzawa, ~¥6,300 each way, seats on Eki-net from Dec 3. JR East usually sells a train + lift bundle ("JR SKISKI") from November.
  • Lift ¥6,000 online. Rental board + boots ~¥5,500, plus jacket/pants if you didn't pack them. Bring your own boots if you're picky.
  • Snow: low elevation, so late-December can be thin and icy. Check SnowJapan and snow-forecast.com the week before; if the base is under 100 cm, swap this day with Jan 4 and go to Shimokitazawa instead.
  • Saturday means crowds. If the group can flex, Monday Jan 5 is quieter but that collides with Uji.
  • Upgrade: same train to Echigo-Yuzawa + 40 min bus to Kagura for tree runs and real terrain, ¥7,500 lift, 2.5 h door to door.

bookshopping

Uji: the matcha pilgrimage (long day)

6:45 leave hotel → Nozomi to Kyoto → local train to Uji → Byodo-in, Nakamura Tokichi, grind your own matcha → back at the hotel ~21:30.
triptrain14-hour day
Uji is the town where Japanese matcha was perfected 800 years ago; it's near Kyoto, 3.5 hours from our hotel each way. The group chose to do it as a day trip rather than sleep away from the suite, so this is a dawn-to-late-evening day with a hard train home. Monday: weekday crowds, everything reopened after New Year.
  • Trains: Shinjuku → Tokyo Station → Nozomi 8:00ish → Kyoto 10:15 → JR Nara Line rapid 20 min → Uji ~10:50. About ¥14,000 pp each way ordinary reserved (Green Car ¥19,000). Book on SmartEX the moment it opens, Dec 5. Right-hand E seats for Mt Fuji about 40 minutes in.
  • Byodo-in (¥700 + ¥300 for the Phoenix Hall interior, timed entries every 20 min): the temple on the ¥10 coin.
  • Nakamura Tokichi Honten: the matcha cafe. Take a numbered ticket from the machine on arrival (a QR code tracks your wait) then go see Byodo-in. Midday waits run 60–90 min. Matcha parfait, matcha soba, hojicha jelly. Itoh Kyuemon and 850-year-old Tsuen Tea by the bridge are shorter-queue backups.
  • Grind your own: Fukujuen Ujicha Kobo, ¥2,200, 40 min, stone-mill tencha into matcha, then drink it. Reserve by phone. Bigger version: Marukyu Koyamaen factory tour, English twice a week, email booking, ask now.
  • Ujigami Shrine (UNESCO, oldest shrine building in Japan, free) and the river walk if time allows. The municipal tea house Taiho-an is reportedly closed until Jan 9, so it's out.
  • Home: Uji 17:00 → Kyoto → Nozomi 17:50 → Tokyo 20:05 → hotel 21:30. Dinner is an ekiben on the train, which is its own Japan experience.
  • If the group wants Fushimi Inari's torii tunnels: it's on the same local line, two stops from Kyoto. Adds 90 min; something else has to go.

Street karts, Akihabara, latte art

Go-kart Tokyo's streets in costume → electronics and retro games → latte art class → cocktails in Ginza.
IDP neededflex day
Recovery from Uji, and the day for the two "class/experience" items. Nothing here is time-critical, so it's also the buffer if something earlier slips.
  • Street go-karts (the "Mario Kart" thing, minus the Nintendo license since a lawsuit): 1–2.5 h, ¥11,000–20,000 pp, costume included, routes past Tokyo Tower and over Rainbow Bridge. Hard rule: you need a 1949-Geneva International Driving Permit from AAA (~$20), issued in the US before you fly. No permit, no kart, no exceptions. Real traffic, real karts.
  • Akihabara: Yodobashi Akiba (nine floors of electronics), Super Potato for retro games, Mandarake. (Yodobashi's Nishi-Shinjuku flagship is by the hotel for anything practical.)
  • Latte art class for Elijah: GetYourGuide's "Discover Coffee Experience" is 90 min, English, max 4, on real machines. Or Koffee Mameya Kakeru's coffee-omakase counter for the tasting version.
  • Cocktails: Bar High Five (Ginza, the classic), Gen Yamamoto (Azabu-Juban, seasonal tasting flights, 8 seats), The SG Club (Shibuya). Reserve; ¥1,000–1,500 cover is normal.

Tokyo DisneySea (group vote)

The adult Disney park: Fantasy Springs, Tower of Terror, drinks in the park → last dinner in Shinjuku.
tickets ~Nov 7vote
DisneySea exists only in Tokyo. It's the one Disney park designed for adults: nautical theming, alcohol served, Fantasy Springs (Frozen, Tangled, Peter Pan lands opened 2024). Japanese school break ends about now so crowds drop from the Dec 29–Jan 3 peak.
  • Tickets ¥7,900–10,900 depending on date tier, sold only on the official site, released about two months ahead, so roughly Nov 7 for this date. Popular dates sell out.
  • Fantasy Springs rides need a free Standby Pass from the app (grab it at park opening, they run out) or paid Premier Access ¥2,000–2,500 per ride.
  • Open to close is ~9:00–21:00; 45 min from Shinjuku by train.
  • No Disney: a free Tokyo day. Yanaka old town, Kichijoji and Inokashira Park, Kagurazaka, second onsen, a last big meal.

Tax refund, then home

Late checkout → Narita by 14:00 → tax refund desk → JAL 62 at 17:05 → LAX at 09:40 the same day.
departure
  • Tax-free changed on Nov 1, 2026. Old system: show passport, skip the tax at the register. Food, and what gets booked when

Budget from Justin: about $250–300 per person per fine-dining night, roughly ¥37,000–45,000. That comfortably covers Tokyo's best two-star tasting menus. Michelin Tokyo 2026 lists 160 starred restaurants; nearly all of them close Dec 30 → Jan 3, so our windows are Dec 29, Dec 30 and Jan 4–7. Proposal: three booked nights (Dec 29, Dec 30, Jan 4), everything else casual.

The one argument for going over budget: a top sushi counter (Kanesaka, Hakkoku, Tokami) runs ¥40,000–50,000 and is a genuinely different experience from anywhere in the US. If we do one splurge, that's the one. Den and Florilège fit the budget as-is.

用語
First-timer cheat sheet

Words that show up above

Shōgatsu正月 — Japanese New Year, Jan 1–3. The country's biggest holiday; family time, shrine visits, everything closed.
Hatsumode初詣 — the first shrine visit of the year. Pray, toss a coin, buy a charm, pull a fortune.
Joya no kane除夜の鐘 — temple bells rung 108 times at midnight on Dec 31.
Bonenkai忘年会 — "forget the year" work parties. Every izakaya is packed the last week of December.
Fukubukuro福袋 — sealed lucky bags sold Jan 1–3 at big discounts. A national sport.
Osechiおせち — the traditional New Year meal in lacquer boxes, ordered weeks ahead.
Izakaya居酒屋 — a Japanese pub: small plates, beer, highballs, loud.
Yokocho横丁 — an alley of tiny bars and food counters (Omoide, Golden Gai, Nonbei).
Depachikaデパ地下 — the basement food hall of a department store. Best takeaway in the world.
Konbiniコンビニ — 7-Eleven, Lawson, FamilyMart. Open 24/7, food is genuinely good, ATMs work.
Omakaseお任せ — "I leave it to you." The chef decides the courses. How sushi counters work.
Kaiseki会席 — a multi-course seasonal Japanese tasting menu.
Onsen / sento温泉 / 銭湯 — hot-spring bath / neighborhood bathhouse. Wash first, no swimsuits, no phones. Most ban tattoos; ours don't.
Shinkansen新幹線 — the bullet train. Nozomi is the fastest Tokyo–Kyoto service. Reserve one month ahead.
Suicaスイカ — tap-to-pay transit card. Add to Apple/Google Wallet before flying.
IDP — International Driving Permit, 1949 Geneva version, from AAA in the US. Required for the street karts.
Tocho都庁 — the Tokyo Metropolitan Government Building, next to our hotel, with free observation decks.
Comiket — the world's largest comics convention, Dec 29–31 at Tokyo Big Sight. Avoid Odaiba those days.
Tax-free — from Nov 1, 2026: pay tax in store, refund at the airport before departure. Keep receipts.

## 1. Mainstream attractions + smarter alternatives

### Shibuya Sky vs. free alternatives
- **Shibuya Sky** (Shibuya Scramble Square rooftop, 229m): Adult ¥2,700 (entry before 3pm) / ¥3,400 (after 3pm), online ¥300 cheaper than door. Timed-entry ticketing.
  - Online tickets release ~2 weeks (14 days) ahead, around midnight JST.
  - Sunset slots (~1hr before sunset — best, catches daylight→golden hour→dark) are the most popular and sell out fastest: weekday/off-season sunset slots are gen. bookable 2–3 weeks out; weekend/high-season sunset slots can sell out within hours of release. Morning/early-afternoon slots rarely sell out.
  - Given Dec 26–Jan 8 sunset ~16:30 (very early), and NY period is a high-demand window, **book sunset slot the moment it opens** (~14 days ahead — i.e. check exact release date, likely ~Dec 12 for a Dec 26 visit).
  - Site: shibuya-scramble-square.com/en/sky (JS-heavy, fetch failed directly — verify current pricing before trip).
- **Free alternatives:**
  - **Shibuya Hikarie 11F Sky Lobby** — free, no reservation, open 7:00–24:00. Great elevated view over Shibuya crossing/skyline. Best low-effort option, especially at night.
  - **Tokyo Metropolitan Government Building (Tocho) observation decks** — FREE, 202m up, right next to the Hyatt Regency (walking distance in Nishi-Shinjuku). South deck 9:30–22:00 (last entry 21:30); North deck 9:30–17:30 (last entry 17:00); each has different closed days — check calendar day-of. Best "free Shibuya Sky substitute" given proximity to hotel.
  - **Cé La Vi** (Shibuya Scramble Square 47F rooftop bar) — no cover charge to enter as paying customer (food/drink minimum), alternative sunset view with a cocktail instead of ticketed observatory.

### teamLab: Planets vs. Borderless (Azabudai Hills)
- **teamLab Planets** (Toyosu): Tickets from ~¥3,200–3,600 depending on date (weekday/weekend pricing). Free for kids ≤3. Visit takes 60–120 min. Water-based exhibits — wear shorts/roll-up pants, no shoes (they provide/require barefoot in water areas). **Note: scheduled to close end of 2027** — this may be one of the last chances to see it. Timed entry required.
- **teamLab Borderless** (Azabudai Hills, Minato): Adult (18+) ¥3,600–5,600 depending on date; ages vary ¥1,500–2,800; free ≤3. Hours 10:00–21:00. Recommend ~2 hrs.
- **Smarter pick given "last chance" framing:** Planets is the one disappearing (closing end of 2027) — prioritize it if choosing one. Borderless is newer/larger and less time-pressured.
- Book via official site (teamlab.art) — timed tickets, book ahead especially for NY period.

### Tsukiji Outer Market vs. Toyosu
- Tsukiji **inner wholesale market moved to Toyosu in 2018**; Tsukiji **Outer Market** (retail/food stalls) remains and is still a great walk-through food destination — but many stalls close during New Year holidays (see Section 2).
- **Toyosu Market** has the tuna auction viewing (advance/lottery reservation for close-up viewing gallery, free general observation deck) and sushi restaurants (e.g., Sushi Dai-style shops relocated there). Best for the "real" wholesale market experience + early breakfast sushi. Also check Toyosu's holiday closures around NY.
- Smarter alt: go early (market areas busiest/freshest before ~9am); Outer Market for casual snacking/shopping, Toyosu for the auction + higher-end sushi breakfast.

### Senso-ji
- Go at ~7am to beat tour-bus crowds; temple grounds open 24/7 (main hall/Nakamise shops open later, ~9-10am+). Free entry.

### Meiji Jingu
- Free entry to shrine grounds; forest walk. Very crowded for hatsumode during NY period (see Section 2).

### Tokyo Skytree vs. Tokyo Tower
- Skytree: taller (350m/450m decks), pricier, farther from center (Sumida). Tokyo Tower: more central, cheaper, retro charm, shorter lines typically.
- Smarter alt: if budget-conscious or short on time, Tokyo Tower or free options (Tocho, Hikarie) over Skytree.

### Akihabara / Ginza / Harajuku-Takeshita / Shinjuku Golden Gai & Omoide Yokocho
- Standard free-roam districts — no tickets needed. Golden Gai/Omoide Yokocho: many tiny bars charge a seating/cover fee (¥300-1500) on top of drinks; some are cash-only and locals-only unfriendly to walk-ins — go with a local-friendly bar guide or look for "tourist welcome" signage.

### Ghibli Museum (Mitaka)
- **CRITICAL TIMING:** Tickets go on sale on the **10th of the month at 10:00am JST**, for the **following month only**, via Lawson Ticket. Sells out within minutes, especially weekends/holidays.
  - For a **Dec 26, 2026** visit → tickets go on sale **Nov 10, 2026, 10am JST**.
  - For any **Jan 2027** dates (Jan 1–8) → tickets go on sale **Dec 10, 2026, 10am JST**.
  - Must convert to local time and be ready at the exact moment; have backup date/time choices; expect site slowdowns/crashes.
- This is one of the most time-sensitive bookings in the whole trip — flag for calendar reminder.

### Imperial Palace East Gardens
- Free entry, but closed Mondays, Fridays, and **around New Year (check exact closure dates for Dec 2026–Jan 2027)** — East Gardens typically closed Dec 28–Jan 3ish; confirm exact dates closer to trip **[UNCERTAIN — verify]**.

### Yanaka / Kichijoji & Inokashira Park / Shimokitazawa / Odaiba
- All free-roam neighborhoods, no special tickets. Inokashira Park has a pond/boat rentals (small fee). Odaiba has teamLab-adjacent attractions, Gundam statue, Palette Town area, DiverCity mall — good half-day, especially with kids-free crowd since this is an adults trip.

## 2. New Year period (Dec 29 – Jan 3) — CRITICAL

### Closures
- **Museums**: Many close for an extended holiday block. Example: Tokyo National Museum closed Dec 26–31, 2026; Jan 1, 2027 status TBD; museums generally start reopening Jan 2, more by Jan 3. **Assume most museums/galleries closed Dec 29–Jan 1, check individual sites for exact reopen dates.**
- **Tsukiji Outer Market**: many stalls close for several days around New Year (some vendors take Dec 31–Jan 3ish off) — verify specific shop hours closer to date.
- **Department stores (Ginza etc.)**: Closed Jan 1; reopen with limited/shortened hours Jan 2–3 (e.g. Ginza Mitsukoshi: closed Jan 1, 10am–6pm Jan 2, normal hours resume Jan 3).
- **Restaurants**: Many independent restaurants close for several days (common "shogatsu yasumi" closure, often Dec 30/31–Jan 3); chains and hotel restaurants generally stay open. Book/verify restaurant hours in advance for this window.
- **Banks/ATMs**: closed Jan 1–3 (some 7-Eleven/convenience store ATMs still work).

### What's open / special events
- **Joya no Kane** (Dec 31 night): temple bells rung 108 times at midnight — Senso-ji and other major temples participate; free, very crowded near midnight.
- **Hatsumode** (New Year shrine visits): Meiji Jingu and Senso-ji are Tokyo's top hatsumode spots — expect **massive crowds**, especially Jan 1 (first 3 days peak, "sanganichi"). Meiji Jingu draws ~3 million visitors over the New Year period, among Japan's most-visited hatsumode sites — arrive very early morning or late evening to reduce crowding, or go Jan 4+ for a much calmer visit.
- **Jan 2, 2027 — Emperor's New Year Greeting** at Imperial Palace: annual public event, Their Majesties + Imperial Family appear on the palace balcony ~5 times between roughly 10:10am–2:20pm (exact 2027 schedule not yet published — historically confirmed each Dec by the Imperial Household Agency, kunaicho.go.jp). Free, security screening, enter via main gate into East Plaza; free paper "Hinomaru" flag given to wave. **[UNCERTAIN — exact 2027 appearance times not yet officially announced as of Sept 2026; check kunaicho.go.jp closer to date.]**
- **Fukubukuro** ("lucky bags"): sold Jan 1–2 at most department stores/malls — big shopping draw, lines form early morning Jan 1/2 at major stores (Shibuya, Shinjuku, Ginza).
- **Comiket 109**: confirmed **Dec 29–31, 2026** at Tokyo Big Sight. Notable: expanded back to 3 days after 7 years of 2-day events; East Halls 4-6 unavailable due to venue renovation (limited space). Circle exhibits 10:30–16:00 (17:00 for corporate booths days 1-2, 16:00 day 3). This falls squarely within your trip dates — huge nearby crowds/hotel demand around Odaiba/Ariake if visiting Big Sight area.
- **Shibuya Scramble crossing NYE**: The organized countdown event has been **cancelled again** (following 2019 ordinance issues) — city is actively discouraging crowds from gathering at Shibuya Station/crossing on NYE, with fencing/extra security/police coordination. Alternative official event: "Happy New Year Tokyo" at Tokyo Metropolitan Government Building — projection mapping, live performances, countdown just after midnight (free, and conveniently right by your hotel).
- **Hatsuhinode (first sunrise, Jan 1)**: Popular Tokyo-area spots:
  - Tokyo Skytree — special early opening ~5:30am for Jan 1 sunrise viewing, advance tickets often required.
  - Tokyo Tower — first-come basis, ~60 people let in starting 2am Jan 1.
  - Free/no-ticket alternatives outside the city: Miura Beach or Yokohama's Osanbashi Pier (Boso Peninsula sunrise over Tokyo Bay), Mt. Takao (sunrise ~6:50am + hatsumode combo at Yakuoin Temple, 50 min from Shinjuku).
- **Winter illuminations still running early Jan 2027**:
  - Marunouchi: mid-Nov 2026 – Feb 15, 2027 (Gyoko-dori Christmas market runs only Nov 28–Dec 25, but tree lights continue).
  - Tokyo Midtown: mid-Nov 2026 – late Feb 2027 (ice rink closed Jan 1, 2027).
  - Yebisu Garden Place: early Nov – **late Dec 2026 only** — likely ENDED before Jan 2027, confirm exact end date **[UNCERTAIN]**.
  - Shinjuku Southern Terrace ("Minamillumi"): seasonal, similar Nov–Jan window historically — confirm 2026/27 dates closer to trip **[UNCERTAIN]**.
  - Meguro River "Minna no Illumi": early Dec 2026 – late Jan 2027 — good pick, still running for your whole trip.

## 3. Tokyo Disneyland / DisneySea (Dec 26–Jan 8)

- **Crowds**: This window includes NY holidays (very high crowds Dec 29–Jan 3) tapering into a busy-but-more-manageable period Jan 4–8 (still winter school holidays in Japan, so still crowded, not low-season).
- **Ticket on-sale timing**: Tokyo Disney Resort tickets go on sale via the official site at set windows — official pattern confirmed for special-category tickets released "2:00pm" JST on a set date (e.g., College Passport opened 2pm Nov 13, 2025 for spring 2026 dates; Shuto-ken Weekday Passport opened 2pm Feb 6, 2026). Standard 1-Day Passports are generally released roughly 2 months ahead of the visit date, sold only through the official TDR website reservation/purchase page. **Given your trip is Dec 26–Jan 8, expect standard-date tickets to open ~2 months ahead (i.e., roughly late Oct/into Nov 2026) — watch the official site's ticket calendar closely and buy the moment your dates open, since NY-period tickets sell out.**
- **1-Day Passport price tiers (2026, 4-tier date-based pricing)**:
  - Adult: ¥7,900–¥10,900 (NY period will likely be at/near the top tier).
  - Junior (12–17): ¥6,600–¥9,000.
  - Child (4–11): ¥4,700–¥5,600.
- **Disney Premier Access (DPA)**: paid line-skip, ¥1,500–¥2,500 per attraction per person, priced/released daily via the Tokyo Disney Resort app on morning of visit (not pre-bookable in advance online).
- **Fantasy Springs (DisneySea)**: As of 2026, the land itself has open/free walk-in access (no gate scan/Standby Pass needed just to enter the area). To actually ride the 4 headline attractions (Anna & Elsa's Frozen Journey, Rapunzel's Lantern Festival, Peter Pan's Never Land Adventure, Peter Pan's Never Land Adventure) you need either a free Standby Pass (issued via app after park entry, limited quantity, can run out early) or paid DPA (~¥2,000–2,500/ride).
- **Which park for adults with no kids**: **DisneySea** is generally recommended for adult travelers — more sophisticated theming, better food/alcohol options (it serves alcohol, Disneyland does not), more thrill-oriented/adult-appeal attractions (Fantasy Springs, Journey to the Center of the Earth, Tower of Terror, Soaring). Disneyland is more classic/kid-centric.
- **NYE special event**: For the "2026" New Year's season, Tokyo Disney Resort's holiday event runs **Jan 1–12** with parade-route New Year's Greeting shows (~25 min, ~21 performers), decorations, fireworks, special menus — but there is **no separate NYE countdown/lottery ticketed event on Dec 31** (that type of special ticketed NYE event has not been offered in recent years per search results). **[UNCERTAIN — confirm whether this repeats identically for Dec 31, 2026/Jan 2027 season closer to the date via tokyodisneyresort.jp/en]**.

## 4. Street go-karting ("Mario Kart" street karts — Street Kart / MariCar-style tours)

- **International Driving Permit (IDP) requirement**: Japan only accepts IDPs issued under the **1949 Geneva Convention**. US drivers must obtain this **before leaving the US** — it cannot be obtained in Japan or from a US embassy there. Get it from **AAA** (~$20 USD), valid 1 year from issuance. Bring your regular US driver's license too (required alongside the IDP).
- **Price**: Tours typically run **¥11,000–¥20,000 per person** depending on route/length/operator (roughly $75–$135 USD). Popular operators: Street Kart (kart.st / streetkart.com), plus similarly-branded competitors (Kartzilla, JapanKart, Monkey Kart).
- **Duration**: Typically ~1–2.5 hours including costume rental, briefing, and the drive itself; routes commonly pass Tokyo Tower, Roppongi, Shibuya, Odaiba/Rainbow Bridge area.
- **Reputation**: Legitimate and popular but note — due to Nintendo's past lawsuit, operators can't use official Mario costumes/branding anymore (no more literal "Mario Kart" costumes at some operators, though some still offer costume rental generically). Reviews generally positive as a novelty experience; safety caveat — you drive real go-karts on public roads with other traffic, no seatbelts/helmets on some vehicles, so it carries genuine road risk. Book in advance, especially for winter dates.
- **Action item**: Since IDP must be obtained before departure, get this done **well before Dec 2026** if planning this activity — AAA offices, need passport photo + existing license.

## 6. Weather & sunset (late Dec 2026 / early Jan 2027)

- **Temperatures**: Daytime highs trend from ~13°C (55°F) in early/mid Dec down to ~9°C (48°F) by late Dec; January daytime highs ~8°C (47°F), overnight lows can dip to ~-1 to 4°C (30–39°F). Cold but not brutally so — pack layers, a proper coat, gloves/scarf recommended especially for evening outdoor activities (illuminations, sunrise-watching).
- **Rain**: December and January are among Tokyo's **driest months** — roughly 5–6 rainy days in December, ~14% daily precipitation chance in January, total rainfall very low (~50-60mm in Dec). Snow is rare in central Tokyo (under 1% chance in Dec) but not impossible in Jan.
- **Sunset**: Around **16:30 (4:30pm)** in this window — genuinely one of the earliest sunset periods of the year, meaning "sunset observatory" slots (Shibuya Sky, etc.) will be scheduled quite early afternoon/mid-afternoon, and outdoor evening plans should account for full darkness by ~17:00.

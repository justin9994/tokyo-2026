Context: 4 adults, Hyatt Regency Tokyo (Nishi-Shinjuku). Brittany loves matcha; group wants a dedicated Uji day. Trip window includes New Year (Dec 29–Jan 3), which is the WORST possible window for this — flagged throughout.

## 1. Logistics Tokyo → Uji

### Shinkansen: Tokyo/Shinagawa → Kyoto
- **Nozomi**: ~2h10–2h15 direct, fastest option. Fare (per person, one way, ordinary reserved seat): **¥13,970–¥14,170** (sources vary slightly; SmartEX fare chart is authoritative — check at booking). **Green Car**: **~¥18,840–¥19,040** (~¥4,900–5,000 premium over ordinary).
- Peak-season surcharge of **+¥400** applies during New Year period (this trip qualifies, Dec 28–Jan 6ish typically) — small but real.
- **JR Pass note**: Since the Oct 2023 reform, JR Pass holders CAN ride Nozomi/Mizuho by paying the ordinary-Hikari-to-Nozomi fare difference as a supplement (~¥4,900–5,000/ride for Tokyo–Kyoto) — pass alone does not give free Nozomi access. Hikari/Kodama are fully covered, only ~5 min slower than Nozomi on this route.
- **JR Pass economics for this trip**: 7-day ordinary pass = **¥50,000/person** (price increase scheduled Oct 1 2026 — confirm current price close to travel). A single Tokyo↔Kyoto round trip costs ~¥27,000–28,000 by itself. **The pass does NOT pay off for one Kyoto round trip** — you'd need to add Hiroshima, Kyushu, or several more long-distance legs to break even. **Recommendation: buy individual Nozomi tickets via SmartEX, skip the JR Pass**, unless the itinerary includes other long-haul rail days elsewhere in Japan.

### Kyoto → Uji
- **JR Nara Line** (rapid/"Miyakoji Rapid"): Kyoto Station → JR Uji Station, **~17–20 min rapid** (some sources say up to 20 min) or ~30 min local, flat fare **¥240** one way. This is the faster, more direct option and the one to use.
- **Keihan Uji Line** (alternative, useful if coming from Gion/Kawaramachi side): JR Nara Line Kyoto→Tofukuji (¥150, ~3 min) → transfer to Keihan Main Line → Chushojima → transfer to Keihan Uji Line (all-local, 8 stops, ~15 min) to Keihan Uji Station. Total ~35–40 min, ~¥360. Keihan Uji Station is a short walk from Byodo-in via Uji Bridge — slightly more scenic approach but slower/more transfers than JR.
- **Verdict on JR vs Keihan**: JR Nara Line rapid is faster and simpler for a straight Kyoto Station start. Keihan is worth it only if you're coming from central Kyoto (Gion/Kawaramachi) without detouring to Kyoto Station first.

### Door-to-door from Shinjuku (Hyatt Regency Tokyo)
- Hyatt Regency Tokyo → Shinjuku Sta (JR/subway, ~10 min) → Tokyo or Shinagawa Sta via Chuo/Yamanote line (~15–20 min) → Nozomi to Kyoto (~2h10–15) → JR Nara Line rapid to Uji (~20 min) + transfer buffer.
- **Realistic door-to-door estimate: ~3.5–4 hours one way**, not ~3h — budget transfer/walking/ticket buffers at Shinjuku, Tokyo/Shinagawa, and Kyoto Station. Return trip same length.

### Verdict: True day trip vs. overnight
- A same-day round trip from Shinjuku (≈7–8 hrs of pure transit for a ~4–6 hr window in Uji) is **physically doable but punishing**, especially in late December (sunset ~16:30–16:45 in Kyoto/Uji, so you lose the last daylight hour) and worse with 4 adults coordinating luggage-free day-trip logistics. Given the trip already spans two weeks with no fixed onward commitments implied, **recommend 1 night in Kyoto** (or Uji itself) to de-risk queue times at Nakamura Tokichi and allow an early Byodo-in visit before crowds, plus add a Kyoto half-day.
- **Option A — 1 night in Kyoto** (recommended): hotels near Kyoto Station (Kyoto tower area / Hotel Granvia Kyoto attached to station, or Karasuma-Shichijo area) minimize the final Nara Line hop. This also lets you slot in Fushimi Inari (on the Nara Line route toward Uji — literally on the way) as a dawn add-on.
- **Option B — 1 night in Uji**: fewer lodging choices, but you wake up already there, beating tour-bus crowds at Byodo-in and the Nakamura Tokichi queue (arrive at 9:30–9:45 for a 10:00 open). Trade-off: less to do in Uji itself in the evening (most shops close 17:00–18:00), and you'd need a side trip into Kyoto city anyway for dinner/nightlife.
- **Option C — true day trip** (if schedule is fixed and can't add a night): feasible only if you (a) take an early Nozomi (leave hotel ~7:00, board Nozomi ~8:00–8:15), (b) accept a hard cutoff to catch trains back before ~18:00–19:00, and (c) treat Nakamura Tokichi as a "get in line early or skip it" gamble.

**Sample Timeline — Option A (overnight Kyoto, recommended)**
- Day 1: Hyatt Shinjuku depart ~8:00 → Nozomi ~8:45 Tokyo/Shinagawa → arrive Kyoto ~11:00 → check into Kyoto Station-area hotel, drop bags → lunch near station → afternoon in Kyoto (Nishiki Market or Kiyomizu) → dinner in Kyoto.
- Day 2: Up early, JR Nara Line rapid Kyoto→Fushimi Inari (~5 min) for dawn torii-gate walk (~1–1.5 hr) → back to Nara Line, continue to Uji (or backtrack to Kyoto Sta and re-board — Fushimi Inari and Uji are both on the same JR Nara Line, so no backtracking needed, just stay on/re-board) → arrive Uji ~9:30 → line up at Nakamura Tokichi Honten (~9:45) while others visit Byodo-in (opens 9:00 for garden, Phoenix Hall interior tours 9:30–16:10) → group reconvenes for Tokichi seating → afternoon: Fukujuen Ujicha Kobo matcha-grinding (book ahead), Tsuen Tea, Ujigami Shrine, Uji Bridge stroll → early dinner in Uji or back in Kyoto → Nara Line back to Kyoto Sta → Nozomi back to Tokyo (last comfortable departure ~19:00–20:00 to be back at hotel by ~22:30).

**Sample Timeline — Option C (true day trip from Tokyo)**
- 7:00 depart hotel → 8:00–8:15 Nozomi from Tokyo/Shinagawa → 10:25–10:30 arrive Kyoto → 10:35 JR Nara Line rapid → ~10:55 arrive Uji.
- 11:00–13:00 Byodo-in (garden + Hoshokan; Phoenix Hall interior tour if timed slot available) and immediate queue-ticket pickup at Nakamura Tokichi.
- 13:00–14:30 lunch/matcha at Tokichi (once seated) or Itoh Kyuemon if queue too long.
- 14:30–16:00 Fukujuen Ujicha Kobo grinding experience (pre-booked slot) or Tsuen Tea / Ujigami Shrine / Uji Bridge.
- 16:00 walk back to JR Uji Sta, 16:20 Nara Line to Kyoto, 16:45 arrive Kyoto Sta, buffer, 17:15–17:30 Nozomi back to Tokyo, arrive ~19:45–20:00, back at hotel ~20:30–21:00.
- This leaves almost no slack for delays, long queues, or a second matcha stop — treat it as a stress-tested minimum, not the ideal plan.

## 2. Uji Itinerary — Sights, Hours, Prices, Closures

**CRITICAL: This trip's dates (Dec 26 2026–Jan 8 2027) overlap New Year, when many Uji attractions have reduced hours or are closed outright. Confirmed/likely closures below — verify again closer to the date since 2026/27 holiday notices typically post ~1 month out.**

- **Byodo-in (Phoenix Hall / Hoshokan Museum)** — UNESCO World Heritage temple, the ¥10-coin building.
  - Garden + Hoshokan Museum: **¥700 adult** (¥600 group), Jr/HS ¥400, Elementary ¥300.
  - Phoenix Hall interior viewing: **+¥300**, timed entries every 20 min, 9:30–16:10, max 50 people/tour.
  - Site states "open every day" for garden/museum. **New Year-specific hours not confirmed from official site in this pass** — Japanese temples commonly keep normal or slightly adjusted hours (no all-day closure expected, but double-check the "News" section on byodoin.or.jp closer to the date, especially for Jan 1–3, since hatsumode-adjacent sites sometimes shift hours).
  - Official site: byodoin.or.jp/en

- **Nakamura Tokichi Honten (Uji main store)** — famous matcha parfait / matcha soba cafe, ~150 years old.
  - No confirmed official hours pulled directly (site page attempts 404'd); third-party listings say roughly **10:00–17:30 daily**. A separate **Byodoin-branch** location also exists near the temple (smaller, sometimes shorter queue).
  - **Queue system**: take a numbered ticket from a machine near opening (~9:45), ticket has a QR code to track wait remotely. Reported waits: ~30–35 min if you're in line by 9:35–9:45 for the 10:00 open; by early/mid-afternoon queues can hit 60+ groups (90+ min); some visitors reported 3–4 hour waits later in the day.
  - **Strategy**: arrive by 9:30–9:45 and queue immediately, before doing Byodo-in, or split the group (one queues while others sightsee).
  - **New Year closure status: UNCONFIRMED.** Many Kyoto-area food businesses close for a few days around Jan 1–3 (shogatsu) even if not officially posted yet for 2026/27 — treat this as a real risk for a Jan 1–3 visit and have Itoh Kyuemon or Tsuen Tea as a backup.

- **Tsuen Tea** — claims to be Japan's oldest tea shop, founded **1160**, right at the foot of Uji Bridge. Good backup if Tokichi's queue is too long; smaller, historically atmospheric.

- **Itoh Kyuemon** — known for elaborate matcha parfaits, generally less of a wait than Tokichi; good alternate lunch/dessert stop.

- **Fukujuen Ujicha Kobo (matcha-grinding stone-mill experience)**
  - **Price: ¥2,200** (tax incl.), ~40-minute class grinding tea leaves on a stone mill, followed by tea made from your own ground matcha + a sweet.
  - Hours 11:00–16:00, **last admission 15:00**; reservation preferred (phone 050-3152-2930), priority given to reservations, walk-ins accepted if space allows.
  - Address: 10 Uji Yamada, Uji-shi (Ujicha Kobo 2F, 1st Workshop). Portal: experience.fukujuen.com

- **Taiho-an (Uji City Municipal Tea Ceremony House)** — ⚠️ **HIGH-PRIORITY FLAG**
  - Sources conflict slightly on price (some say ¥500–1,000 historically; more recent listings show **¥1,500 usucha/sencha, ¥1,800 gyokuro, ¥3,000 gyokuro+sencha set, ¥4,000 koicha+usucha set, ¥3,600 hands-on otemae course**) — confirm current pricing on kyoto-uji-kankou.or.jp before planning.
  - Hours generally 10:00–16:00.
  - **One source states Taiho-an is closed annually from December 21 through January 9** — if accurate, **Taiho-an would be fully closed for the ENTIRE Tokyo trip window (Dec 26–Jan 8)**. This is not yet cross-confirmed on the official page (the official kyoto-uji-kankou.or.jp/taihoan-en.html page references a closure-dates PDF that wasn't captured in this pass). **Treat Taiho-an as likely unavailable for this trip and do not build the itinerary around it** — verify directly by phone (0774-23-3334) or the official PDF before finalizing, but plan on it being closed.

- **Ujigami Shrine** — one of Japan's oldest extant shrine buildings, UNESCO-listed alongside Byodo-in; free, always-open shrine grounds, quick 10–15 min visit, a short walk from Byodo-in across the river.

- **Tale of Genji Museum (Genji Monogatari Museum)** — small museum on the "Uji chapters" of the Tale of Genji; typically closed Mondays (standard Japanese museum pattern) — **verify exact closure day given a Monday could fall inside the trip window; also confirm no year-end/New Year closure**.

- **Mimurotoji** — temple famous for seasonal flowers (hydrangea/azalea in season); in winter (Dec–Jan) there's little in bloom, so this is a **lower-priority stop for this specific trip timing** — best skipped in favor of tea-focused stops unless the group wants the temple architecture/quiet itself.

- **Uji Bridge / Ujigawa (Uji River)** — free, scenic, historic bridge (one of Japan's oldest bridge sites), good for photos and the walk between JR Uji Station side and Keihan Uji Station side; Tsuen Tea sits right at its base.

### Best days to visit
- **Avoid weekends** (heavier tourist + domestic day-tripper crowds) and **absolutely avoid Jan 1–3** (New Year "shogatsu" — many food/retail businesses reduced hours or closed, and this is separately when Kyoto's biggest shrines see hatsumode crowds, indirectly pushing more day-trippers toward secondary spots like Uji too).
- Given the trip spans Dec 26–Jan 8, the **best weekday windows** are **Dec 28–30** (before New Year closures fully kick in, but check Dec 29–31 too, since some shops close a few days pre-New Year) or **Jan 5–8** (after New Year closures lift, before the following weekend). Recommend targeting a weekday in the **Jan 5–7** range if flexible, since more businesses (Taiho-an per the Dec 21–Jan 9 closure claim, possibly Tokichi) should be fully reopened by then, versus the higher closure risk still lingering Dec 26–30.

## 3. Matcha Classes/Experiences (English) — Uji & Kyoto

- **Fukujuen Ujicha Kobo** (Uji) — see above, ¥2,200 stone-mill grinding, 40 min, English signage/support common for tourists at this well-trafficked spot. Also offers a longer "Uji Tea Making Experience Course" and an "Enjoy! Uji Matcha Full Course" per experience.fukujuen.com — worth checking those two variants for a longer/more comprehensive session if the group wants more than the basic grinding class.

- **Marukyu Koyamaen Factory Tour** (Uji) — one of Japan's most respected matcha producers.
  - Offers guided tours **twice a week in English**, ~90 minutes: introductory film + factory tour showing matcha production, plus a visit to a traditional tea hut where you prepare and drink your own bowl of matcha with a wagashi sweet.
  - Booking is via email through their website (not a walk-in/instant-book system) — **book well in advance** given only 2 English slots/week and a 4-person group.
  - Price not confirmed in this pass — check directly when emailing to book.

- **Kanbayashi Shunsho** — 450+ year old Uji tea house/producer, historically significant, but **no dedicated public tour or English-language visitor experience was found** in this research pass. Likely retail-only storefront rather than an experience; if the group wants to visit, treat it as a shop stop, not a booked activity, unless further direct inquiry turns up a tour.

- **d:matcha Wazuka Tea Farm Tour** (Wazuka, ~45–60 min from Uji/Kyoto by car or shuttle) — full-day organic farm tour + tasting, entirely in English.
  - **Price: ¥35,000/person**, ~4 hours (9:30 AM–1:30 PM).
  - Shuttle: departs 8:45 AM sharp from **Ishiyama Station** (North Gate), ¥1,500/person one-way, no advance reservation needed for the shuttle itself but arrive by 8:30 AM.
  - Booking: direct through dmatcha.com only (not via travel agencies); free cancellation up to 7 days before, final/non-refundable inside 7 days.

Given it's billed as a farm/harvest experience, **there's a real risk this tour doesn't run in late Dec/early Jan (dormant season)** — email info@dmatcha.com directly to confirm before counting on this for the trip. At ¥35,000/head (¥140,000 for 4 people) this is a major spend, so confirming availability first is important.
  - Note: Ishiyama Station is on the Keihan/JR Biwako side (Shiga side), not directly from Uji — factor in extra travel time/cost if combining with the main Uji day rather than doing it as a separate excursion from Kyoto.

**Overall matcha-experience recommendation for Brittany**: Fukujuen's grinding class (cheap, easy, on the main Uji day) + Marukyu Koyamaen's English factory tour (book now given only 2 English tours/week) are the highest-value, lowest-risk picks. Treat d:matcha Wazuka as a "nice to have" only after confirming winter operation — don't lock it into the plan yet.

## 4. Shinkansen Booking Practicalities

- **SmartEX** (smart-ex.jp/en) — the official Tokaido/Sanyo/Kyushu Shinkansen reservation portal for foreign travelers.
  - Accepts foreign-issued **Visa, Mastercard, Amex, JCB, Diners**; occasional declines happen due to overseas-transaction security holds on some cards — have a backup card ready.
  - Reservations open **1 month ahead** of travel date.
  - Delivers a **QR-code ticket** (can be saved to Apple Wallet) scanned directly at gates — no physical ticket pickup needed if using QR entry; alternatively pick up a physical ticket at a station machine using your pickup code + the card used.
  - Discount fares exist for advance booking (e.g., EX Hayatoku 21 / EX Hayatoku 7 tiers) — worth checking if the Kyoto dates are locked in 21+ days out, as these often beat standard Nozomi fare.
  - Alternative: **Klook** also resells Shinkansen tickets/JR Pass exchange orders with English support and sometimes bundles — compare against SmartEX direct pricing, but SmartEX direct is usually cheapest and gives the most seat-selection flexibility.

- **Luggage rules**: Standard reserved seats fit normal carry-on/medium suitcases in overhead racks or between-seat space at the back of some cars. **Oversized luggage (any single dimension sum over 160cm, up to 250cm)** requires a **free advance "oversized luggage" seat reservation** (JR calls this the ōkina nimotsu / large-luggage reservation) — reserve this when booking on SmartEX/at a ticket office; without it, oversized bags can be refused or you'll be asked to move. With 4 adults on a multi-week Japan trip, at least some suitcases will likely qualify — plan to reserve those seats specifically (usually the last row of a car, seats with rear luggage space).

- **Best seats for Mt. Fuji views**: Sit on the **right-hand side of the train (seats A or E depending on car orientation, but commonly referenced as "E seats") heading FROM Tokyo TOWARD Kyoto/Osaka** — Fuji is visible on the right side of the train in that direction. Visible roughly **40–45 minutes after departing Tokyo**, near **Shin-Fuji Station**, weather permitting (best on clear winter mornings, which favors this trip's late-Dec/early-Jan timing). Request/select these seats when reserving on SmartEX's seat map.

## 5. If Overnighting in Kyoto — High-Impact Half-Day Add-Ons

- **Fushimi Inari Taisha** — the single best pairing with an Uji day: it sits directly on the **JR Nara Line between Kyoto Station and Uji** (Inari Station, ~5 min from Kyoto Sta), so a dawn visit costs almost no extra transit time/money if timed right. Go at **dawn (~7:00 AM)** to see the famous torii-gate tunnels without crowds, then continue on the same line toward Uji.
  - **New Year crowd warning**: Fushimi Inari draws roughly **2.7–2.8 million visitors during Jan 1–3 hatsumode**, with peak crush around **Jan 1, 10:00 AM–4:00 PM**, and lines described as kilometers long during peak days. **If any part of the trip falls in Jan 1–3, only visit Fushimi Inari at dawn (~7:00 AM) on Jan 2 or 3** — never mid-day Jan 1–3, and ideally do it on a non-New Year day entirely if the schedule allows (e.g., Dec 27–30 or Jan 5+).

- **Nishiki Market** — covered food-market street in central Kyoto, good half-day (or even 1–2 hour) stop for snacking/shopping, easy to combine with Gion.

- **Gion** — historic geisha district, best explored early evening (dusk) for atmosphere; good pairing with a Kyoto-Station-adjacent hotel night before or after the Uji day.

- **Kiyomizu-dera** — hillside temple with sweeping Kyoto views, one of the most iconic Kyoto sights; expect meaningful crowds during New Year period as well (though nowhere near Fushimi Inari's hatsumode scale) — a morning visit is calmer than afternoon regardless of exact dates.

**Suggested half-day combo if overnighting in Kyoto**: dawn Fushimi Inari → continue same line to Uji for the full tea-focused day → return to Kyoto in early evening for Gion/Nishiki Market dinner and stroll before heading back to Tokyo (or the next morning if staying a 2nd night).

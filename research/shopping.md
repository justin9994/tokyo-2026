## 0. CRITICAL: TAX-FREE SYSTEM CHANGES NOV 1, 2026 — DIRECTLY AFFECTS THIS TRIP

- **New model = refund-at-departure ("Refund Method")**, like South Korea/EU/Australia. You **pay full price including 10% consumption tax at checkout**, get a purchase record/receipt, then get the tax refunded **after customs confirms at the airport** that the goods are being taken out of the country (at departure, not on arrival).
- **No parallel/transition period** — the switch is a hard cutover on Nov 1, 2026.
- **Simplifications**: the old split between "general goods" and "consumables" (food/cosmetics, previously required special sealed tamper-proof bags, no opening until leaving Japan) is being **abolished**. The ¥500,000/day purchase cap is also being **abolished**.

- **Practical impact for your trip**: for every tax-free-eligible purchase (watches at Nakano/Ginza dealers, denim, streetwear, knives, electronics at Yodobashi), keep every receipt physically — likely you'll register at a refund counter/kiosk or via a QR/app system at Narita/Haneda before departure. ### Pricing realities, JDM models, authenticity culture, bargaining, tax-free mechanics
- **Pricing vs US**: Japan-market watches (especially Japanese brands) can run **~20–30% cheaper than US retail**, sometimes more on entry models, though gaps narrow sharply on flagship pieces. Example found: Seiko 5 SNZG15K1 = ¥21,000 in Japan vs $149 in US (roughly comparable once converted — savings modest at street level); Grand Seiko SBGH281 = ¥530,000 in Japan vs $5,100 in US (~7% difference at time of search — i.e., NOT dramatically cheaper on high end due to currency/duty effects). Bottom line: don't assume huge Japan discounts on Grand Seiko/Seiko flagship pieces vs US retail in 2026 — the yen-to-USD gap has narrowed compared to a few years ago; check current exchange rate close to travel. For Rolex/Omega (Swiss brands), Japan pricing is generally NOT cheaper than US and can be similar or higher at authorized dealers — the real value in Tokyo is the **breadth and quality of the used/vintage secondary market**, not new-MSRP arbitrage.
  Sources: [Extrabux — Cheapest Seiko country guide](https://www.extrabux.com/en/guide/7318683), [WatchCharts Grand Seiko Sept 2026 pricing](https://watchcharts.com/watches/brand/grand+seiko)
- **JDM (Japan-domestic) models**: Seiko sells many dial variants, limited editions, and entire sub-lines (Seiko 5 Sports JDM colorways, certain Presage/King Seiko variants, Grand Seiko boutique-exclusive dials) that are Japan-only or Japan-first. Ask dealers specifically for "Japan limited" or "boutique limited" pieces if that's of interest.
- **Authenticity/grading culture**: Japanese dealers (JackRoad, Shellman, Komehyo, Quark, Ishida, etc.) are known for rigorous internal grading and strong emphasis on completeness — "box and papers" (箱・保証書, hako/hoshosho) significantly affects price and is tracked meticulously. Movement authentication and service history are typically disclosed up front. This is a major reason Tokyo is considered one of the best vintage-watch markets globally — condition grading tends to be conservative/honest by reputation (though as always, verify independently for high-value purchases).
- **Bargaining**: Very little to no haggling culture at fixed-price watch dealers in Japan — prices are typically firm, unlike flea markets. Occasionally minor negotiation on very high-ticket pieces or multi-item purchases, but don't expect discounts as a default; asking politely for "any discount available?" (少し安くなりますか) is not offensive but often gets a polite no.
- **Tax-free mechanics for watches (post-Nov 1, 2026 reform — see Section 0)**: watches count as "general goods" (durable, non-consumable) — under the old system these could be opened/used immediately in Japan (unlike sealed consumables). Under the NEW system starting Nov 1, 2026, the general/consumable distinction is being abolished entirely, so the process should be uniform: pay full price + 10% tax at purchase, then reclaim the tax at the airport before departure, regardless of item type. Given watches are high-value, keep every single receipt.

- **Nintendo Tokyo** — also inside Shibuya Parco (same building as Pokémon Center, different floor) — convenient combined visit.

### Art/design books & lifestyle flagships
- **Tsutaya Ginza** — art book–focused branch inside Ginza Six (mentioned above).
- **Daikanyama T-Site (Tsutaya Books flagship)** — architecturally famous multi-building bookstore campus in Daikanyama, with curated design/art/travel/food sections, a lounge, and adjoining cafés — one of Tokyo's most pleasant slow-shopping destinations, pairs naturally with the Daikanyama fashion stops (Okura, Hollywood Ranch Market, Bonjour Records) listed above.
